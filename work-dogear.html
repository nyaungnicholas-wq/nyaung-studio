Dogear

Ten photos in, one paper-scrapbook reel out

Built

A paper-scrapbook reel template: ten photo slots, 13.7 seconds, 1080x1920, 411 frames at 30fps. Drop ten photos into a folder, run one command, and the reel comes out cut to a fixed beat structure. It was reverse-engineered from a popular editing-app template rather than designed from scratch.

The problem

Templates inside consumer editing apps are manual work. Each photo is dragged into place by hand, and the timing that makes the result read as stop-motion is invisible in the interface. Reproducing one is slow, inconsistent between attempts, and any later change means redoing the edit. To make the look repeatable, the beat grid, the card sizes and the transitions all have to be recovered from the reference and written down as parameters instead of gestures.

What I built

The template renders 411 frames at 30fps from ten input photos and one command. A check script enforces the two invariants that matter: the beats must tile the timeline exactly with no gap or overlap, and every slot from one to ten must appear at least once. Paper sound effects mix over an optional music bed, and a frame-limit flag renders just the opening two seconds in about six seconds of work instead of ninety.

How it works

The reel is a list of beats that tile the timeline exactly, each beat naming a frame range and which photo slot it shows. Four named mechanisms carry the motion: DeckRiffle counts a centred card through the slots on the beat, ZoomFlick hands a card to the next size with a single frame of averaged zoom blur, PaperWad crushes the hero card into a wad of creased paper over three frames, and StripSpread swaps tall strip cards side by side. Photos are centre-cropped to the slot aspect rather than squashed, and a missing slot falls back to a numbered placeholder plate.

Highlights

  • Beats tile the timeline exactly and the check script fails the render if they stop tiling.
  • Four named mechanisms carry the motion: DeckRiffle, ZoomFlick, PaperWad and StripSpread.
  • Photos are centre-cropped to each slot's aspect ratio, never squashed to fit.
  • A missing photo slot falls back to a numbered placeholder plate instead of breaking the render.
  • The reference was measured at roughly 89 BPM, one beat every 0.670s or 20.1 frames at 30fps.
  • A frame-limit flag renders the opening two seconds in about six seconds instead of ninety.
